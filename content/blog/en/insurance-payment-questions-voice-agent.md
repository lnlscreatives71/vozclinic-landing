# 10 Insurance and Payment Questions a Voice Agent Can Answer for You

Money questions are the ones that decide the booking, and they are also the ones that eat your front desk alive. Every cross-border patient wants to know what they will pay and how before they commit to crossing the border, and your team answers the same handful of questions dozens of times a day, in two languages, often at hours when nobody is at the desk. When those answers come slow, the patient books with the clinic that answered fast.

A bilingual AI voice agent takes the repetitive money questions off your team and answers them in seconds, in English or Spanish, from the exact prices and policies your clinic set. It never invents a number, and anything it is not sure about goes straight to your staff. Here are ten of those questions, and how the agent handles each one.

## 1. "Do you accept my insurance?"

This is usually the first question a U.S. patient asks, and the one most likely to stall when the bilingual staff member is busy. The agent answers immediately with the plans and networks your clinic actually works with, in the patient's language, so the conversation keeps moving toward a booking instead of waiting for a callback that may never come.

## 2. "Can you check my coverage before I come?"

Cross-border patients do not want to drive four hours on a guess. Where your clinic enables verification, the agent collects the patient's plan details up front and confirms coverage and eligibility before the visit, so there are no surprises at the counter and no appointments that fall apart when the patient arrives. The heavier claims work stays with your team; the agent handles the intake that starts it.

## 3. "How much will I actually pay out of pocket?"

Patients do not care about the sticker price, they care about their number. The agent walks through the price your clinic set for the service and what the patient can expect to owe, clearly and in their language, so the person on the other end can make a real decision instead of guessing and going quiet.

## 4. "What forms of payment do you take?"

Cash, card, U.S. dollars, Mexican pesos, transfer: every clinic handles this differently, and a patient planning a trip needs to know before they pack. The agent answers with exactly what your clinic accepts, so nobody shows up ready to pay in a way you cannot process.

## 5. "Can I pay in dollars?"

For a San Diego or Phoenix patient, this is a practical trip-planning question, not a small one. The agent confirms whether your clinic takes dollars and how you handle it, in seconds, so a U.S. patient can budget the visit with confidence instead of worrying about currency at the door.

## 6. "Will you give me a receipt I can submit to my insurance?"

Many cross-border patients pay out of pocket and file for reimbursement afterward. Knowing whether they will get an itemized receipt or superbill often decides whether they book at all. The agent answers what your clinic provides, so the patient who plans to claim it back knows the paperwork will be there.

## 7. "Do you offer payment plans or financing?"

For larger cases, the treatment is only real if the patient can spread the cost. If your clinic offers plans or financing, the agent explains the options your clinic set, in plain language, so a patient who would otherwise walk away because of the total sees a path to yes.

## 8. "Is that price all-in, or are there extra fees?"

The fear behind a lot of hesitation is the hidden add-on: lab fees, materials, consultation charges that show up later. The agent gives the honest, complete answer your clinic configured, so the patient trusts the number, and trust in the number is what turns a price question into a booked appointment.

## 9. "Do I need to pay a deposit to hold the appointment?"

Deposits protect your calendar, but only if patients understand them before they book. The agent explains your deposit policy and, where set up, collects or arranges it as part of booking, so the appointment that goes on your calendar is one the patient is actually committed to keeping.

## 10. "Can you send me the estimate in writing?"

A patient comparing three clinics wants something concrete to weigh, and the one that puts a clear estimate in their hands first usually wins. The agent sends the written estimate your clinic approved, right there in the conversation, so your quote is the one sitting in the patient's WhatsApp when they decide.
